# Direct picture (experimental GPU viewer slice)

Status: VP1 of 2 plus the VP2a playback work (10/19), the VP2b overlays and readout (10/20), editing on the direct picture (10/20) and the paused picture and A/B compare on the card (10/22), opt-in, default off. The Viewer (a QGraphicsView fed by `Window._show_image`) is unchanged and remains the default, the fallback, the export source and the pixel-sampling source.

## What it is

Window menu, "Direct picture (experimental GPU)", or start with `NODEBASED_DIRECT_VIEWER=1`. It opens a floating dock holding a `QRenderWidget(present_method="screen")` (`nodebased/directviewer.py`). Every new preview picture the Viewer receives is also offered to this widget. The newest ready picture is converted to RGBA once and written once into a retained wgpu texture; wheel zoom, middle-drag or Alt-drag pan and double-click fit change a small uniform buffer and redraw, with no `to_qimage` and no pixmap rebuild. A format outline and an orange corner mark are drawn in a second GPU pass.

The dock takes the scene-linear float frame instead of the Viewer's 8-bit picture (`submit_linear`), playing or paused, with or without a B buffer, and the card runs the whole display transform and the A/B compare; see "Display transform on the card" and "Paused picture and compare on the card" below. The Viewer's overlays and the pixel readout are painted over the picture (below). A left click or drag on it goes to the same hit-test the Viewer uses (below), so a Roto point, a Tracker square, a Transform handle, the region of interest or the wipe line can be moved there; the dock title says "view only" only when Preferences, "Edit on the direct picture", is off. The dock is not part of the saved workspace.

## Contracts

- **Ownership.** `FrameLedger` holds the rules; `RetainedPresenter` is the only code that creates or destroys GPU objects. A picture that fits the retained texture is copied into it (a smaller one uses its top-left corner; the shader reads only the picture's own size); a larger one grows the texture and the old one is destroyed after the draw that replaced it is submitted. Float pictures keep one texture per format, so a 4K half-float frame and a float32 crop do not replace each other. WebGPU's `destroy()` frees memory only once the queue has completed the submissions that used it, so this is a GPU-completion rule, not a count of presents (VP1 held it for 3 presents). Closing waits for the queue to go idle (`wait_idle`, a 4-byte mapped copy: wgpu-py 0.32's `on_submitted_work_done` cannot be called against wgpu-native 29). Pending pictures are bounded to the newest one; older pending pictures are dropped before conversion.
- **Upload (VP2a).** Pictures go to the card through `StagingRing`: three reused `MAP_WRITE` buffers. When a picture is offered and no other picture is being filled or waiting for its draw, the Qt thread maps the slot the card finished with longest ago and a filler thread copies the picture into it (a float frame as it is, a cropped cache view included; a QImage converted to RGBA8888 there; a float frame over four million pixels cast to half floats on eight threads straight into the buffer). The filler wakes the widget through a queued signal, and the draw only records `copy_buffer_to_texture` before its render pass. One picture sits between offer and copy at a time, so newer offers replace the pending one and no picture is converted only to be dropped. Closing the dock waits for a fill in flight before it unmaps anything.
- **Size limit.** The device asks for the adapter's own `max-buffer-size` and `max-texture-dimension-2d`. A picture wider or taller than the texture limit, or larger than one upload buffer, raises `TooLarge` naming its size and the card's limit; the status bar shows that sentence and the Viewer shows the picture. During playback the worker checks the same limits before it hands a float frame to the card, so an oversized frame is converted on the worker and never on the Qt thread.
- **Retained picture.** The presenter keeps the newest picture only (the CPU copy that device-loss recovery re-uploads). Closing the dock releases it with the device, the textures and the upload buffers; the next picture starts a fresh presenter. The cache inspector shows the retained picture and the upload buffers in megabytes while the dock is open, and "closed, holds no memory" once it is closed.
- **Revision and epoch.** Offers carry the source generation and the device epoch. An older generation or an older epoch is rejected. Equal generations are accepted (playback).
- **Surface textures.** `get_current_texture()` is called inside one draw and nothing from it survives the draw.
- **Pacing.** Input handlers change view state and call `request_draw()`; rendercanvas schedules the draw from Qt callbacks (capped at 120 per second). Nothing spins or waits for a surface.
- **Device.** One device, requested from the adapter wgpu reports as presentable to this window (`request_adapter_sync(canvas=...)`). `get_preferred_format` alone is not enough: on a display server without DRI3 it answers for an adapter whose `configure` aborts the process in native code. `NODEBASED_DIRECT_ADAPTER=discrete|integrated|cpu` states a wish; when the window cannot be presented to by that kind, the presenter uses the one that can and records the reason in `selection_note`.
- **Device loss.** wgpu only logs a lost device (the promise API raises `NotImplementedError`), so `LossWatch` listens to its log for "device lost" and a failing draw counts as a loss too. `RetainedPresenter.recover()` bumps the epoch, destroys every old resource and the old device, makes a new device from the same adapter, configures the surface again and queues the retained CPU picture (or a newer pending one) for upload. The widget repeats the failed draw once on the new device. Three recoveries within 30 seconds make it give up.
- **Failure.** Any start failure, or a draw that still fails after one recovery, hides the dock, unchecks the menu item and shows a status message; the Viewer carries on. Native Wayland is refused at the switch (see gaps) before any surface exists.

## Known gaps (VP1)

- **Overlays and hit order.** Drawn since VP2b (section below). Paint strokes in progress, the input strip and the Viewer's corner HUD (zoom, proxy badge) stay on the Viewer. Since 10/20 the direct surface forwards left-button presses, drags and releases to the Viewer's own hit functions ("Editing on the direct picture", below); with the preference off it handles only wheel zoom, middle-drag or Alt-drag pan and double-click fit and forwards nothing (`HitOrderTests` in test_directviewer.py runs in that mode).
- **Colour and alpha.** The card runs the view transform itself, playing or paused (below). A view or channel it cannot build, and a picture or A/B pair too large for the card, show the Viewer's 8-bit picture instead. Surface formats other than 8-bit (HDR, wide gamut) are not handled; sRGB-format surfaces re-encode, which the shader undoes once (tested within one code value).
- **Playback.** While the direct picture plays, the Viewer holds its last picture; the refresh when playback stops repaints it. Paused, the Viewer takes only the picture's place and size ("Paused picture and compare on the card").
- **Sampling** is nearest at every zoom, like the Viewer; no mip or filter for strong minification. With the desktop scaled 1.75x, Xwayland stretches the window by the compositor, so on this workstation the glass shows the picture resampled; the numbers above are before that stretch.
- **Native Wayland.** The installed rendercanvas (2.7.2) builds only X11 surface ids on Linux; under `QT_QPA_PLATFORM=wayland` the Qt window id is not an X window and the process died with an X `BadWindow` error. The switch now raises "native Wayland is not supported" (`unsupported_platform`) and the Viewer carries on. Run NodeBased with `QT_QPA_PLATFORM=xcb` (Xwayland) for the direct picture; this is what every real-screen number here used. A native Wayland surface needs a rendercanvas/wgpu change, which is outside VP1.
- **Windows** (D3D12/Vulkan backend choice, DWM) and macOS: not run; this workstation cannot.
- **Device loss.** Exercised by destroying the device under the presenter (wgpu then reports "Parent device is lost" on the next call) and by a logged loss message, on the RTX 3080 Ti, the Radeon and llvmpipe; recovery rebuilt the picture from the retained CPU image. A physical event (eGPU unplugged, driver reset) was not provoked.
- **Latency.** Present return is when the application call returns, not screen response. Nothing here measures input-to-photon time. rendercanvas caps drawing at 120 per second, so a wheel event waits up to 8.3 ms for its draw (measured median 8.3 ms to present return) on top of the compositor.
- **Hidden or occluded dock** still receives offers; the newest-only rule bounds the cost to one conversion per drawn frame. A closed dock receives none and holds nothing.
- **Radeon on a private display.** Xvfb has no DRI3, so the Radeon 8060S cannot present to it and a request for it selects the RTX 3080 Ti (recorded in the selection note). The playback-response and frame-order numbers below therefore cover the RTX and llvmpipe; the Radeon's upload times come from the offscreen measurement. Real-display runs are left for an interactive session.

## Display transform on the card

`nodebased/carddisplay.py` builds `to_qimage`'s whole chain as one GLSL 4.50 fragment shader that wgpu compiles through naga: gain, channel select (RGB, R, G, B, A), unpremultiply, gamma, the view transform, re-associate, the checker background, the clipping warning and the 8-bit quantise (the unorm target rounds). The view transform is OCIO's own GPU shader for the view, the same text gpudisplay gives OpenGL; OCIO's lookup data (for ACES 2.0 the reach and gamut-cusp tables, nearest-sampled at texel centres, and a 363-entry hue array) goes into one read-only storage buffer per view; inlined as constant arrays it made the ACES draw 17 ms instead of 0.4 ms, because naga copies a constant array for every dynamic index. A view needing a 2D or 3D lookup, or a filtered table, raises `Unsupported`, and that frame goes through `to_qimage` as before.

The frame is uploaded once into an `rgba32float` texture (32 MB at 1080p, about 1.9 ms); settled half-float cache frames go up as `rgba16float` as they are. A float16 conversion on the processor costs 11 ms at 1080p, more than the upload it would save.

`tests/test_carddisplay.py` holds the card to `to_qimage` within 1/255 per channel over linear ramps, HDR highlights to 200, slightly negative values, alpha edges and premultiplied noise, for every channel mode, the sRGB, ACES 2.0 and Linear views, the checker, exposure, gamma and the clipping warning. Checked on the RTX 3080 Ti, the Radeon 8060S (force-adapter.py integrated) and llvmpipe (cpu). The largest difference seen is one code value.

`Window` hands the dock a `carddisplay.LazyPicture` (the float frame, the display settings and, with a B buffer, B and the compare mode) instead of `to_qimage`'s result, playing or paused. The 8-bit picture is built only if something asks for it: an open output preview, the dock closing, or the card refusing the view or the pair.

## Measurements

Tools: `tools/viewer_direct_bench.py` (cadence, conversion and upload counts, process CPU), `tools/viewer_direct_functional.py` (dock walk), `tools/viewer_direct_screen.py` (what is on the glass). Results: `benchmarks/viewer/direct-vp1-*` (private Xvfb display, 10/18 first pass) and `benchmarks/viewer/direct-vp1f-*` (this workstation's real display, KDE Plasma on Wayland with Xwayland `:0`, 2194x1234 logical at 120 Hz, desktop scale 1.75, 10/18 finish pass).

**On the glass.** `viewer_direct_screen.py` shows a changing checker, takes a Spectacle screenshot of the composited desktop and measures the checker in it. On all three adapters, at pixel ratio 1.0 and 1.5: the picture is visible; each new picture reaches the screen with the right top-left colour (blue, orange, blue); the cell size matches the view (within 2.5 px); five wheel steps grew the cells 1.15^5 within 0.12; a 60 px middle-drag moved the grid 105 px against 104.75 expected (capture pixels per widget pixel 1.7458, the desktop scale); resizing to half and back followed the widget; closing and reopening showed the picture again. Spectacle captures every window the compositor shows; X captures do not see a screen-presented surface.

**Cadence and cost, 1920x1080 pictures, Viewer and direct widget the same size** (the Viewer pinned to a 1920x1080 viewport, 240 pictures and 240 wheel steps each):

| | Viewer (default) | Direct, Radeon | Direct, RTX 3080 Ti | Direct, llvmpipe |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| pictures per second | 154 | 121 | 107 | 121 |
| CPU per picture | 4.7 ms | 2.8 ms | 9.4 ms | 61 ms |
| CPU per wheel step | 3.6 ms | 1.4 ms | 2.2 ms | 56 ms |
| picture interval p50 / p95 | no pacing | 8.3 / 8.9 ms | 9.3 / 9.5 ms | 8.2 / 8.9 ms |
| wheel to present return p50 | 1.2 ms (synchronous raster) | 8.3 ms | 8.3 ms | 8.3 ms |

Reading it: the Viewer's repaint is a synchronous Qt raster into the X backing store, so its 154 per second and 1.2 ms say nothing about what reaches the glass; the direct widget is paced by rendercanvas at its 120 per second cap on this 120 Hz display. What the comparison does support: on the display's own Radeon the direct path uses about 40 percent less CPU per new picture and about 60 percent less per pan or zoom step, converts and uploads each picture once and never again on a view change (counters in the JSON), and shows the picture exactly. The eGPU draws correctly but pays for crossing USB4 (9.4 ms of CPU per picture), and llvmpipe is a fallback that costs far more than the Viewer. The 8.3 ms wheel response is the 120 per second cap, not the work. Whether the artist feels it as faster is not measured here.

The first-pass Xvfb numbers (no GPU presentation) stay in the repository and describe that display server, not a screen.

## Playback (VP2a, 10/19)

Tools: `tools/viewer_direct_playback.py` (the real Window on a private X display, a Checker with an animated cell size playing at 24 fps with the dock open, real `xdotool` presses and drags on the graph and drags on the frame bar, each timed from the moment xdotool is told to act to the end of the next paint, plus a 4 ms timer on the Qt thread), `tools/viewer_direct_upload.py` (one picture at a time from hand-over to on the card, no window), `tools/viewer_direct_screen.py --playback` (each played frame carries its number as a strip of cells; `tools/x_frame_probe.py` reads the strip off the window as it plays). Results in `benchmarks/viewer/direct-vp2a-*`. Measured on this workstation only (RTX 3080 Ti over USB4, Radeon 8060S, llvmpipe); the A6000 and the hybrid laptop were not measured.

**What blocked.** Before this step, at 4K the dock's draw took up to 262 ms on the RTX: during playback the Viewer asks for its visible crop, so pictures alternated between a 544x146 crop and the whole 3840x2160 frame, and every size change made a new 133 MB float texture, followed by a 34 to 38 ms `write_texture` on the Qt thread. At 1080p the dock was never the long pole: the Qt thread's longest stalls came from presenting to Xvfb (no DRI3, so the swapchain image is copied out by the processor) and from the Properties panel being rebuilt after a graph press.

## Overlays and the pixel readout (VP2b, 10/20)

**How they are drawn.** The Viewer's foreground painting is one function, `Viewer.paint_overlays`; `drawForeground` calls it with the widget's transform, and `Viewer.render_overlay(view, size, ratio, readout)` calls it with the direct view's transform into a premultiplied RGBA8 image the size of the surface (device pixel ratio set, so a one-pixel cosmetic pen is as wide as on the Viewer at that scale). The Viewer's own rendering hints are used. What is drawn is therefore what the Viewer draws, not a second implementation: the A/B wipe line, centre and turn handles and B's half of the picture; the region-of-interest box with its handles; format masks (lines, half, full) and the overscan edge; Roto shapes, points, tangents, feather and the transform box; Tracker, planar, survey and warp overlays; the Transform handle (box, pivot, ring); Sampler and flare handles; and the "ROI" and "proxy" labels. The image is uploaded to an `rgba8unorm` texture (`RetainedPresenter.set_paint`) and a third pass (`PAINT_SHADER`) draws it one texel per surface pixel with premultiplied blending, after the picture and the existing format outline. Nothing is uploaded when nothing changed: the widget keys the image by view, surface size, pixel ratio and an overlay serial that the Window bumps whenever the Viewer repaints its foreground (every document, selection, drag or compare change does) and whenever the readout text changes. Resize, pan and zoom repaint it because the key changes. After a device loss the texture is gone and the next draw paints it again. Since 10/22 the dock asks for the recorded form (`render_overlay(..., probe=True)`) and a repaint that would give the same image never builds it; see "Overlay uploads" under "Paused picture and compare on the card".

**Same place as the Viewer.** `tests/test_directviewer_overlays.py` renders the Viewer's own widget with and without its overlays at the given scale, lays the card's overlay over the "without" picture and compares with the "with" picture. A pixel agrees when some pixel within one step of it in the Viewer's picture has the card's colour within 16 of 255, because the Viewer's scroll offset is a whole number of logical pixels and an edge on a pixel boundary rounds either way. The corner label strip (text, antialiased differently on an opaque widget than on a transparent image) is compared for ink only. Checked at scale 1.0 and 1.75: wipe (default, and moved and turned), region of interest box, the three mask modes, Roto shapes and points, the Transform box and pivot. The same tests are run once with a real card pass (`CardPaintTests`): an opaque texel lands on the same surface pixel and changes nothing else, a half-transparent one blends with the picture, a stale-size texture is not drawn, an unchanged overlay uploads once. Measured Qt-thread cost of one overlay image on this workstation while other lanes were running (median ms; 1080p at 1.0x, 4K-class 3360 x 1890 at 1.75x): nothing to draw 0.2 / 0.7, wipe 1.1 / 2.5, region and mask 0.8 / 1.7, Roto triangle 2.0 / 6.8. The image is 8 MB (1080p) to 25 MB (3360 x 1890) and goes up only when something changed.

**Hit order.** Resolved on 10/20: the Viewer's press, move and release handling no longer takes a mouse event (section "Editing on the direct picture").

**Pixel readout.** Hovering the direct picture (`hover` on the widget, `Window._direct_hover`) calls `Viewer.pixel_readout_info(scene_point)`, the function the Viewer's own readout now calls too (its text, position, values and A/B buffer are unchanged). It reads `Window.frame`, the scene-linear float array, so a value of 2.5 shows as 2.50000 and not as a display byte. The text is painted bottom left of the surface in the overlay image, with the colour swatch and the buffer tag. While the direct picture plays, the Viewer holds its last picture and its size, tier and region; the played frame's own geometry now comes from the hand-over (`Viewer.readout_geometry`), so the readout does not index a played frame with the held picture's size. Tests: `ReadoutTests` (float value above 1; the Viewer's readout labels equal the shared function's; a played crop is read with its own place; no readout off the picture; readout painted bottom left) and `WindowOverlayTests.test_hovering_the_direct_picture_reads_the_float_picture`.

**Not done.** The overlay is not refreshed per played frame (animated Roto and Tracker handles move when the playback stops and the Viewer repaints); the readout is not updated while the pointer is still and the frame changes; the pointer readout does not follow a wheel zoom until the pointer moves; the format outline from VP1 is drawn on the card in addition to the Viewer's masks.

## Editing on the direct picture (10/20)

**One hit-test for both surfaces.** `Viewer.mousePressEvent`, `mouseMoveEvent` and `mouseReleaseEvent` now only turn the Qt event into a scene point, a button, the modifiers and the pen pressure, and call `Viewer.hit_press`, `hit_move` and `hit_release`; those hold what the three handlers used to hold, in the same order (colour pick and the other pick modes, Tracker, ROI, Roto, warp, paint, analysis region, Transform, flare, the wipe last), and return whether they took the event. The direct picture calls the same functions through `Window._direct_edit` inside `Viewer.hit_surface(zoom)`, which makes `view_zoom()` answer for the direct picture (logical pixels per scene unit, that is the card zoom divided by the device pixel ratio), so every pixel tolerance (for example 12 for a Roto point, 10 for a Tracker square or the Transform ring, 8 for the ROI box, 6 for the wipe line) is the same number of screen pixels on either surface.

**What the direct surface does with the mouse.** Left press, drag and release go to the hit functions (Alt, Ctrl and Shift are passed on; Ctrl+Alt inserts a Roto point as on the Viewer). Middle-drag and Alt+left-drag pan the direct view, the wheel zooms about the pointer and a double click fits, all view-only. Every move is forwarded; it changes something only while a drag the hit-test took is held, or while a Roto or warp shape is being drawn. Whatever cursor the Viewer sets (the closed hand during a drag, the pick cursors) is set on the direct widget too (`Viewer.cursor_sink`), and cleared with it. After every edit the widget repaints its overlay at once, ahead of the Viewer's own repaint. Preferences, "Edit on the direct picture", is on by default; off, the direct picture is view only again and its dock title says so.

**Proof.** `tests/test_directviewer_hit_order.py` builds one document (a Roto node with a curved shape and edge feather, a Tracker, a Transform, a GridWarp and a Flare on a 640 x 360 Constant) and drags each handle kind twice from the same logical pixels: once on the Viewer's viewport and once on the real `DirectViewerWidget` wired by `Window.set_direct_viewer` (only the GPU presenter is left out, so it runs without a display). The 18 kinds are the ROI box corner and edge, a Roto point, a Roto point with feather (Ctrl), a Roto tangent and a feather handle, the Tracker pattern square, search square and point, the Transform centre, pivot (Ctrl), rotate ring and scale corner, a GridWarp pin, the Flare handle and the wipe centre, line and rotate handle. Each runs at device scale 1.0 and 1.75, zoom 0.5, 1 and 3, with the handle 47 by 31 logical pixels off the surface centre, and with the press on the handle and 6 pixels beside it. The document after the drag, the number of undo entries (at most one per drag), the wipe, the Roto and Tracker selection and the cursor must be identical on the two surfaces (floats to 1e-6), and one undo restores the document. The test also checks that the preference turns routing off and the title follows, that Alt-drag, middle-drag, the wheel and double click change only the direct view, and a regression for the bug below. Run time about 110 seconds. The test never creates a GPU object, so the three adapters make no difference to it; it was run under `force-adapter.py` all the same (below).

**A bug the test found.** A drag preview at the proxy tier (tier 8) asked for the visible part of the picture in scene units (full resolution) but clipped it against the tile bounds at the proxy tier, so with the Viewer zoomed 3x onto the lower right the region was empty, `to_qimage` was handed a frame with no columns, the status read "Internal error ... PackedImageDesc Error: Invalid image dimensions", and because the picture was gone `format_rect` was None and every handle stopped working until some later render succeeded. The viewport is now divided by the tier before it is clipped (`Window.start_preview`, `render()`), with `ProxyViewportTests` as the regression test. It affected the Viewer itself, not only the direct picture.

**Feel gate.** `tools/viewer_direct_edit.py`: the real Window on a private X display, the direct dock open with editing on, 25 drags of 8 real `xdotool` moves each (30 ms apart) on a Roto point and 25 on the Transform centre, at 1920 x 1080. Each move is timed from the moment xdotool is told to send it to the end of the first `draw` of the direct picture that painted the overlay after the move reached the shared hit-test, which is the overlay redraw and not the picture's re-render (that follows a few frames later, from the preview tier). Gate: 95th percentile under 50 ms for both handles, and one undo entry per drag. Results in `benchmarks/viewer/direct-edit-*`.

| 1080p, ms (200 moves per handle) | Roto point p50 | p95 | max | Transform centre p50 | p95 | max | undo entries per drag | gate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|---|
| RTX 3080 Ti | 27.8 | 41.0 | 191 | 17.8 | 22.5 | 54 | 25/25 and 25/25 | pass |
| llvmpipe | 14.1 | 47.7 | 194 | 11.7 | 17.0 | 24 | 25/25 and 25/25 | pass |

Both handles gate under 50 ms at the 95th percentile on the RTX 3080 Ti and on llvmpipe, with one undo entry per drag. The RTX medians sit above llvmpipe's because Xvfb copies every presented image back over the USB4 link (the same effect as in the playback numbers above); the Roto point is slower than the Transform centre on both (a heavier overlay and a Roto re-render per move are the likely cause; not isolated). The llvmpipe Roto 95th percentile of 47.7 ms clears the gate by 2 ms: a shorter 6-drag run on the same loaded machine gave 39.9 ms, so treat that margin as thin.

Read these as present return, not light on the glass (see "Latency" above), measured on a private Xvfb display while other lanes were running (load average about 4). The 120 per second draw cap alone puts a median near 8 ms. The Roto tail is the Roto overlay (2 to 7 ms of Qt thread per redraw, measured above) plus the tier-8 preview renders that start on every move and share the interpreter lock with the Qt thread. Not measured: the Radeon (Xvfb has no DRI3, so a request for it selects the RTX), a real display with a mouse, the laptop and the A6000.

**Off-site tiers.** `tools/laptop_present_check.py --edit [--preference low-power] --out DIR` runs the same drags through `SetCursorPos` and `mouse_event` on Windows (xdotool elsewhere) and writes the 50th and 95th percentile per handle, the adapter used, the input driver and the gate into `DIR/result.json` under "edit"; `--read DIR` prints it, and `--self-check` covers the record's fields on a synthetic run. The Windows input driver has never been run; no number from the laptop or the A6000 exists.

**What is not routed or not drawn.** Paint-brush strokes, Roto drawing, Tracker picking and planar picking go through the same functions but are not in the hit-order test, and their in-progress marks (stroke, new Roto points, the rubber band) are not drawn on the direct picture, so drawing a new shape is better done on the Viewer for now. The overlay is still not refreshed per played frame while playing.

## Paused picture and compare on the card (10/22)

**Float at rest.** A frame that is not playing now goes to the card the way a played frame does. `Window._direct_linear_for` no longer asks for playback: with the dock open, a view the card can build and a frame that fits the card, every render (after an edit, on a scrub, on each progressive tier, 1/8, 1/4 and full, and the cached-proxy stand-in) is handed over as a `LazyPicture`, half floats for settled cache frames as before, and `to_qimage` is not run. Each tier replaces the last on the card; the previous texture stays on screen until the copy of the next one is recorded, so no frame is blank. The Viewer behind the dock takes the picture's place and size without a picture (`Window._park_viewer`): the scene rectangle, the corner readout, the format guide, the tier, the render region and the refit rule are updated (the hit-test, the pixel readout and the next render request read them), one black rectangle stands where the picture is, and the scene is rebuilt only when the picture's place changed (refining a tier, or an edit that leaves the frame where it was, only updates the tier and size). Closing the dock, or the card failing, repaints the Viewer from the same float frame (`Window.unpark_viewer`). The 8-bit picture is built when something asks for one: the output preview, the dock closing, a view or channel the card cannot build, or a picture or pair the card refuses. Playback still leaves the Viewer's last picture alone.

**B on the card.** `LazyPicture.with_b(frame_b, mode)` adds the B buffer (already aligned to A's shape) and the compare mode. A and B go up through one staging buffer, B after A at a 256-byte boundary, so they always arrive together; B is a second float texture of its own format (one per format, grown and never shrunk). The shader (`carddisplay.FRAGMENT`) picks per pixel, from the premultiplied floats and before the display transform: A (no compare), B (B only), A or B by the side of the wipe line (wipe), `a + b (1 - alpha a)` (over), `b + a (1 - alpha b)` (under), and `a - b` or `|a - b|` with the larger alpha (minus, difference), which are `compare.combine`'s formulas. The wipe line reaches the shader as a centre and a normal in scene units, read at draw time through `compare_provider` (`Viewer.wipe_line`), so dragging it changes the uniform and uploads nothing. A pixel is on B's side when `(p - centre) . normal > 0`, `compare.wipe_side`'s test, evaluated at the pixel's centre. Since the second pass of 10/22 the card draws the line and its two handles as well (`wipe_marks` in the shader: a 1.5 px gold line, two dark discs of radius 6 with a gold rim and a gold dot, sized in logical pixels times the pixel ratio, drawn after the display transform and over the surround too). The turn handle's place comes from `Viewer.wipe_handle(logical_zoom)` (70 logical pixels along the line), read at draw time with the line, so a drag changes the uniform and nothing else. The Viewer paints neither B's half nor the line for the overlay while `Viewer.card_wipe` is on; its own wipe, on the processor path, still paints all of it. The overlay of a paused compare is therefore the tier badge and nothing else. A "split" is the wipe at 90 degrees (a horizontal line).

`tests/test_carddisplay.py` (`CardCompareAgreementTests`) holds the card to the processor path within 1/255 per channel for B only, wipe, over, under, minus and difference, through sRGB, ACES 2.0 and Linear, the checker, the clipping warning, gamma, exposure and the A, G and RGB channel modes, on three pairs of frames (one with a half-float B), plus the wipe at five positions and angles, a wipe drag that uploads nothing, a picture without B after one with B, and the B texture being reused. Checked on the RTX 3080 Ti, the Radeon 8060S (force-adapter.py integrated) and llvmpipe (cpu); the largest difference is one code value.

**Memory and limits.** A and B together must fit the card's buffer limit, each must fit the texture side limit, and B must be A's size. `directviewer.pair_too_large` and `RetainedPresenter.check_pair` raise `TooLarge` naming the pair, its megabytes and the card (for 4K half-float frames on a card limited to 100 MB: "Direct picture: the A and B pictures are 3840 x 2160 pixels each (127 MB to upload together) and Example card takes at most 100 MB at once; the Viewer shows them"). The worker checks the same limits before it hands a pair over, builds the 8-bit pictures on the processor when the pair does not fit, and says why in the status line ("compare on the Viewer: ..."). The cache inspector's Direct picture line adds "B texture N MB" while a compare is on the card, and the retained picture it reports includes B.

**Overlay uploads.** The overlay image (the Viewer's foreground painted at the surface's size) is no longer built or sent whole each time the Viewer repaints. `Viewer.render_overlay(..., probe=True)`, which the dock uses, first records the same painting into a `QPicture` (commands only, no pixels, microseconds). A recording with no drawing command and no readout answers `EMPTY_OVERLAY`; a recording identical to the last one for the same view, surface size, pixel ratio and readout answers `SAME_OVERLAY` (a card that holds nothing to keep, after a clear or a device recovery, asks again with `force`). Neither builds the 4 MB image. Otherwise the image is painted into one of two reused buffers (the presenter keeps the last image it was given as the base of its next diff, so the other is free), and `RetainedPresenter.set_paint` skips the empty scan for an image the recording said has content, finds the rows that differ, and sends only those rows in bands, and within each band only the columns that differ (a tier badge is about a hundred texels wide), as copies recorded into the next draw. The card keeps the last texture while the overlay is empty, so a tier badge that comes and goes costs a few rows. Before this, a paused dock sent the whole 1280 x 760 image (3.9 MB) up to five times per edit; `render_overlay` without `probe` still returns the image always, as the pixel-parity tests read it.

**Cost gate.** `tools/viewer_direct_bench.py --paused` edits a Grade knob on a 1920 x 1080 frame in the real Window and records, per edit, the Qt thread's CPU time (`time.thread_time()`) and the whole process's, for the Viewer alone (dock closed), the dock with the pre-10/22 hand-off (8 bits built for the Viewer and offered to the card, first round only) and the direct picture, each with and without an A/B wipe. The figure the gate reads is the Qt-thread time of putting the picture up (`Window._show_image`, the Viewer's paint, the dock's draw and overlay paint); the rest of an edit (the command, the graph and panel refresh, the status line) is the same on every path and is reported as the whole-edit time. `--offscreen-card` draws the dock into an offscreen texture and leaves the surface present out: on a private Xvfb display the Vulkan present copies every image out through the processor (100 to 160 ms of Qt-thread time per edit on the RTX), which a real screen does not do. `--rounds N` alternates the paths (Viewer, then the dock opened and measured, N times) and gates on the pooled edits, with the saving of each round beside it: one run of a few tens of edits moved by more than the margin from one run to the next on this shared workstation (the Viewer's own median went from 6.7 to 8.5 ms between runs, and a burst of other work doubled single rounds).

What this pass changed on the direct path: the recorded overlay answers empty or same without building an image, the two reused overlay buffers, the scan and compare it no longer repeats, the column bands above, and (second pass) the wipe line and handles drawn by the card.

**The first figures of 10/22 were wrong and are replaced.** The bench moved the Grade knob through 97 values in a loop, and a run is 124 edits or more, so later rows repeated values that the evaluator had already settled and served them from its frame cache: no render, fewer tier pictures, and a direct path that looked 34 percent cheaper than it is. The bench now never repeats a value (`1.0 + 0.00037 * serial`). Picture path, Qt thread, milliseconds per edit (median of 100 edits per path: four rounds of 25), RTX 3080 Ti, 1080p, final code, two runs; every edit renders and shows four tiers:

| | Viewer | direct picture | direct against Viewer | per round |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| no B, run A | 6.65 | 7.04 | 6 percent more | -3 to -9 percent |
| no B, run B | 6.66 | 7.12 | 7 percent more | -1 to -17 percent |
| with a wipe, run A | 8.61 | 6.58 | 24 percent less | 24 to 27 percent, one round hit by a burst |
| with a wipe, run B | 8.65 | 6.33 | 27 percent less | 27 to 29 percent, one round hit by a burst |

**The 30 percent gate is not met on the RTX: without B the direct picture costs 6 to 7 percent more Qt-thread time than the Viewer, with a wipe it saves 24 and 27 percent.** Where the direct path's 7.2 ms goes without B (per edit, four tier pictures and about five draws): the dock's draws 3.4 (0.65 ms of wgpu calls each: encoder, pass, three pipelines, uniform check, submit), the overlay 1.4 (the recording of the tier badge on every draw, 0.7 of painting and 0.6 of `set_paint` when the badge changed), the hand-off `submit_linear` and `park_viewer` 1.5, the Viewer's parked repaint 0.9. The Viewer path is 6.7 ms (3.7 for four `_show_image` and 3.0 for its paints). With a wipe the Viewer pays for B's pixmap as well (8.6) while the direct path stays at 6.3 to 6.6, which is where the saving comes from. What would close the no-B gap is fewer Qt-thread milliseconds per tier picture: draw on the filler thread instead of the Qt thread (about 3.4 ms), fold the format outline pass into the picture pass, and draw the tier badge from a small cached texture instead of recording and diffing the overlay. None of the three is built. The worker no longer runs `to_qimage` (four to seven runs and 70 to 130 ms of worker time per edit before; zero now). Process CPU per edit and the whole-edit Qt-thread time (46 ms for the Viewer, 59 to 200 ms with the dock open, bursty from run to run) are not claims: the whole-edit figure includes the harness's event-loop spin and was not isolated. `create_buffer_with_data` on this eGPU is slow for megabyte-sized data (about 17 ms for 3.9 MB), which is why the overlay goes up in small bands.

**Laptop stand-in.** `QT_SCALE_FACTOR=2 taskset -c 0-3` with the dock on the Radeon (`--adapter integrated --offscreen-card --rounds 3`; the Radeon cannot present to Xvfb, so the surface is left out), 75 edits per path, picture path in milliseconds, two runs after the bench fix: without B Viewer 8.7 and 9.1 against direct 8.5 and 8.7 (3 and 5 percent less); with a wipe 11.7 and 12.0 against 9.3 and 9.3 (20 and 23 percent less). That row misses the 30 percent figure with and without B. The real laptop, with its own Iris Xe, is not measured.

**Wipe drag.** `tools/viewer_direct_edit.py --handles wipe` drags the wipe's centre handle 25 times (8 real xdotool moves each, 30 ms apart) with B on the card, timing each press and each move from the moment xdotool is told to send it to the end of the first `draw` that shows the moved overlay, and checks that a drag leaves no undo entry (the wipe is view state). With the line and handles drawn by the card, 1080p, RTX 3080 Ti, real surface on Xvfb, final code: moves p50 7.7 ms, 95th percentile 12.5 ms, max 13.2 ms; presses p95 11.8 ms (before the line moved into the shader: moves p95 24.4 ms, presses p95 11.9 ms). The 50 ms limit holds. The time to the end of the surface present is recorded beside it in the JSON (on Xvfb it adds the copy-out: moves p95 38.5 ms). The wipe is dragged with the plain Constant viewed: a Transform's pivot sits at the middle of the format where the wipe starts, and the hit order gives the Transform the press first.

**Off-site tiers.** `tools/laptop_present_check.py --paused [--preference low-power] --out DIR` runs both measurements on the laptop and on the A6000 machine (the cost bench and the wipe drag, in child processes) and writes the wipe press and move times, the Viewer-against-direct figures and the saving into `DIR/result.json` under "paused"; `--read DIR` prints it and `--self-check` covers the record's fields on a synthetic run. The Windows input driver and the laptop's adapters have not run it: no number from either machine exists.

**Not done.** The 30 percent Qt-thread gate on the RTX (met with a wipe in neither run, 24 and 27 percent; without B the direct picture is 6 to 7 percent costlier than the Viewer) and on the laptop stand-in; the three changes named under "Cost gate" would be the next steps. The real-display and Wayland runs. The Radeon's own wipe drag (Xvfb has no DRI3 for it). Interim stand-in pictures are skipped while a B buffer is chosen (a stand-in without B dropped the wipe line for a frame on each edit), so scrubbing over played frames with a compare on shows the full render only. B rendered by a node that does not tile still comes from the processor path of the evaluator, as A does.

## Laptop check kit

`tools/laptop_present_check.py` folds `briefs/proposed/W1-laptop-present-check.md` into the field kit. `--self-check` runs on any machine (it ran here, on Linux, importing neither Qt nor wgpu) and checks the statistics, the arguments, the matrix order (DX12 first, Vulkan as the comparator) and the result record on a synthetic run. On the Windows laptop the matrix runs four 60-second processes and each writes `result.json` (adapter used, backend asked for and reported, class match flag, frame interval p50, p95 and max and frames per second for a held and for a changing picture, input-to-present-return proxy) beside `metadata.json` and `frames.json`; the matrix folder's own `result.json` holds every case, and `--read DIR` prints it on any machine. Steps for DiMo are in `docs/FIELD_TEST.md`. No Windows number exists yet; none can be measured here, and the script marks its records `measured_on_windows` from the platform it ran on.

## Can the direct picture become the default Viewer? (decision memo, 10/20)

**Answer: not yet, and the default is not flipped.** Everyday pan, zoom, playback to 4K, the overlays and the readout now work on it on this workstation's three adapters. What still stops it, and what would close each:

- **Windows is unmeasured.** DX12, Vulkan, DWM and the hybrid laptop (Iris Xe driving the panel, the 4070 as the second adapter) have no run. Closes with: DiMo runs `laptop_present_check.py --matrix` on the laptop (ten minutes) and Gonzo reads `result.json`; then the A6000 machine through the field kit. The default needs a recorded steady cadence on the laptop's own panel at 175% scale.
- **Native Wayland is refused.** rendercanvas 2.7.2 builds only X11 surface ids, so the direct picture runs only under Xwayland. A Wayland-default desktop falls back to the Viewer. Closes with: a rendercanvas/wgpu release with a Wayland surface, or shipping NodeBased with the xcb platform forced; neither is in this repository's control.
- **HDR and wide-gamut surfaces are not handled.** The shader writes 8-bit-range values and undoes sRGB re-encoding; a 10-bit, float or HDR surface needs its own output transform and a colour check against the Viewer. Closes with: surface-format selection in the presenter plus `test_carddisplay` style agreement on a 10-bit target.
- **Click editing** is closed for every handle kind in the hit-order test ("Editing on the direct picture"). Paint-brush strokes and the Roto-drawing, Tracker-picking and planar-picking modes go through the same functions but are not in that test, and their in-progress marks are not drawn on the direct picture.
- **Overlays do not animate during playback**, and the readout waits for the pointer. Closes with: bump the overlay serial per played frame when a Roto, Tracker or Transform context is active (about 2 to 7 ms of Qt thread at 1080p to 4K-class per bump, measured above) or paint the overlay on the filler thread.
- **Paused picture and compare** are on the card since 10/22 (section "Paused picture and compare on the card"): the float frame goes up after every edit and on every settled tier, and the wipe, B only, over, under, minus and difference run in the card shader. What remains is in that section.
- **4K on an external-GPU link.** 171 of 241 frames reached the window at 4K on the RTX through Xvfb; the real-screen run is owed, as are the Radeon on a real display (Xvfb has no DRI3 for it), the hot pour comp, the A6000 and the laptop. Closes with: an interactive session on a real display, which the field kit's A6000 and laptop runs also cover.
- **Resample under desktop scaling.** With 1.75x scaling Xwayland stretches the window, so the glass shows the picture resampled; native pixels need the Wayland surface above.
- **Smaller items.** No mip or filter for strong minification; a picture larger than the card's texture or upload limit is refused by name and the Viewer shows it; the dock is a floating window and not part of the saved layout; latency to photons is unmeasured on every machine.

The Viewer stays the default, the fallback, the export source and the colour-picking source until the first three are closed.
