## Generative video, swept 10/12

**2026-10-12 · Lane 3, T1 · Reading guide: ranking, evidence table, then decisions.**

### Ranked next moves

1. **Wan2.1 VACE-1.3B: first experiment.** Exported depth plus a reference image, joint video denoising, an existing diffusers pipeline and Apache-2.0 weights make this the shortest integration experiment. Its exact controlled workload on our card remains unmeasured.
2. **Wan2.1-Fun V1.1: strongest adjacent control option.** Separate depth/reference and camera checkpoints exist. The small camera model is particularly relevant to our exported poses. Do not assume its camera and depth checkpoints can be combined without implementation work.
3. **LTX-Video: fast reference-video alternative.** Small quantized models have a published consumer-card result. Depth IC-LoRA belongs to a larger model; that small-model result does not prove depth-control fit.
4. **HunyuanCustom / FramePack: low-memory reference experiments.** Useful identity and temporal methods, with a different stack and more restrictive base-model terms. They do not directly consume our scene lighting or camera matrices.
5. **Watch camera-aware methods and LTX-2.x.** ReCamMaster, CameraCtrl II, GEN3C and newer Wan camera/motion variants offer useful mechanisms. Read their control contracts before spending a step on heavier checkpoints.

#### The camera-control line

- **CameraCtrl** (paper 2024-04-02 [K]): camera intrinsics/extrinsics become per-pixel Plücker rays; temporal attention learns viewpoint changes. - **MotionCtrl** (2023-12-06 [K]): separates camera extrinsics from object trajectories. This matches our need to distinguish a dolly from a moving cube. Custom stack; repo Apache-2.0 plus underlying checkpoint terms. Near-size frame-count/speed/12 GB controlled fit unverified.
- **ReCamMaster** (paper 2025-03-14; code 2025-04-09 [R]): conditions on a source video and target camera sequence, preserving dynamic timing. ### 2. What could beat the current pipeline

**Temporal coherence — joint video denoising, with geometric controls.** VACE's shared spatiotemporal representation can coordinate texture and newly visible regions across the clip instead of repeatedly resampling a 2D canvas [V,D]. Depth anchors silhouettes; a reference anchors appearance. That is a plausible way to reduce the current softening/edge ghosting while lowering **2.6 / 7.7**, but no cited benchmark measures our shot. The renderer itself scores **0.19 / 0.30** in stage 8, demonstrating that a lower flicker number alone can reward simply retaining the layout. Require the requested rainy-street change and recognisable objects as well. Cost: the VACE-controlled peak and 3080 Ti runtime are unknown; sequential offload trades host RAM/transfers for VRAM. Apache-2.0 is straightforward, but fixed seeds still require a byte-repeat test. Avoid TeaCache, adaptive fallback or a new attention kernel in the first comparison: each introduces another numerical configuration.

**Lighting consistency — preserve shading evidence, not only depth.** A reference-conditioned video model can keep one appearance through time, yet a consistently wrong key light will still fail the verifier. Depth does not encode light direction, environment colour or shadow placement. The strongest low-cost hypothesis is to condition on the renderer's beauty/shading through masked video editing, or generate albedo once and shade it with the exported lights (section 4). VACE supports the former, but that is a different input experiment from depth-only conditioning [V]. HunyuanCustom preserves subjects, not physical lighting [H]. Cost: a beauty guide may resist the requested restyle; albedo needs de-lighting and materials. Neither establishes light-intensity correctness, which the present verifier explicitly leaves unchecked. Compare its actual direction, shadow and colour-balance observations, marking unavailable checks as unavailable. Model memory/licence costs follow the chosen row; renderer-only subsequent frames avoid repeated diffusion and offer a much simpler determinism contract.

**Camera adherence and objects — camera rays or geometry-backed reprojection.** CameraCtrl encodes rays, MotionCtrl separates camera and object movement, and ReCamMaster conditions video on new camera trajectories [K,R]. GEN3C goes further: render a persistent 3D cache from the requested pose, then complete missing content [G]. Our exported intrinsics, metric geometry and motion remove much of their depth/pose-estimation uncertainty. Fun's small camera checkpoint is the practical learned-camera follow-up [F]. Cost: camera-coordinate conversion, relative-pose/scale normalization and non-rigid object motion still need validation; learned control is approximate, and GEN3C's measured memory is outside our budget. Preserve the verifier's **1 cm / 0.25° / 0.1°** position/rotation/FOV gates from the conditioning contract. Object tracking may also reject changed appearance or ambiguous edges; inspect those matches before attributing a failure to geometry. A camera-conditioned model must be measured from its generated pixels; copying input depth/IDs or declaring camera support cannot substitute for that evidence. Licence and stack changes remain those in the landscape.

### 3. One worker-sized spike

[**VACE depth + reference against stage 8**](../briefs/proposed/T1-vace-depth-reference.md) specifies one model, one fixed offload strategy, aspect-preserving depth/reference preparation, two fresh runs and a 90-minute hard stop. It compares against the exact benchmark, requires better flicker without sacrificing the requested edit or worsening verification, and falls back to the unchanged current provider. This brief is proposed, not authorized execution. In particular, VACE's edit mask and our reprojection-validity mask have different meanings; the experiment keeps them separate.

### 4. Outside the box: three small tests

**Renderer light probes as a shading constraint.** Render neutral diffuse and glossy probe spheres under the exported lights/HDRI, alongside the scene's diffuse-only shading. Fit a small, temporally shared colour/shading correction to the generated sequence, keeping object masks and silhouettes fixed; test first on the existing stage 8 output, with no new model. Compare light-direction, shadow and colour-balance errors plus flicker before/after. This is a proposed experiment, not an existing provider capability. A colour correction cannot move an incorrect shadow: if the direction/shadow checks remain wrong, stop and use the shading render as an explicit future model condition. Cost is a renderer-pass adapter and a constrained fit; avoid calling it physical relighting without that evidence.

**Geometry-guided test-time correction with an appearance anchor.** Freeze the generated frames and optimize a low-resolution residual field using our depth/ID-valid backward reprojection correspondences, penalizing temporal disagreement, edge movement and departure from the original pictures. Start with a bounded CPU test on frames 1–4; preserve high-frequency detail outside the residual field. Use a differentiable surrogate for reprojection error: the production verifier's tracker, masks and scorecard are not an end-to-end differentiable loss. Evaluate all 24 frames afterward using the unchanged verifier and compare unoptimized edges/appearance. This tests the geometry-cache principle behind GEN3C [G] without its large model; the main failure mode is an over-smoothed metric winner, which the image gate must reject.

**Generate material once, render the shot deterministically.** For the existing cube/sphere, project one generated reference onto their known UVs (or a small view-independent atlas), then render every frame using the original geometry, camera and lights. Begin with the non-emissive diffuse component; remove baked shading before treating colour as albedo, and leave unseen texels at their original material. Test whether object/camera matches and light direction survive while texture appearance changes; separately inspect seams, unseen surfaces and specular highlights. Only atlas creation is generative; repeated renders should reproduce identical bytes on the pinned renderer. This is the strongest route to literal camera/lighting locks, but a single projection cannot supply an unseen rainy street or correctly de-light arbitrary material. A one-object proof is enough for the first test.

### Baseline and evidence boundary

### 2. What can improve the current solve

**A sub-percent recovered focal length is an experiment target, not a property promised by any model above.** It is attainable on observable data: DroidCalib's TartanAir example [R20] demonstrates it. A near-straight dolly, planar scene, pure rotation, unknown crop, zoom or rolling shutter can leave focal/depth/distortion coupled. Fixing metric scale with one distance does not in itself remove that coupling. Measure focal in pixels after accounting for resize/crop; convert to millimetres only with the same known film back. Do not use a learned metric scale as proof of absolute position accuracy.

#### Lens: mechanism, incremental change and honest cost

1. **Finish the existing all-frame consistency experiment.** `nodebased/lensrefine.py` already minimizes robust reprojection over shared log-focal, radial terms, keyframe poses and points; `select_observations` caps the problem at **120 frames / 3,000 points**, and the three-stage Huber schedule is **24, 6, 1.5 px at 960-wide** (stage-7 L2, 2026-10-11). The current demo fits within the frame cap. Start by measuring this merged implementation against the exact saved stage-9 inputs; another network is not necessary to learn whether the bias is a missing refinement or an unobservable shot. Improve constraints through well-spread static tracks, longer feature lifetimes, coverage near image edges, and a shared fixed lens across the clip. Do not free principal point, tangential distortion and focal simultaneously on a weak shot. Cost: CPU optimization, track storage and dense reduced pose solves; wall time and host RAM unmeasured on real observations. Larger problems require sparse/block methods rather than simply removing caps. The CPU refinement is repeatable for fixed inputs, but it cannot repair DPVO's nondeterministic input tracks retroactively.

2. **Put the survey inside bundle adjustment.** Current survey handling fits scale/placement after triangulation. Add residuals for observed, fixed-world survey landmarks *during* the focal/pose fit, with weights from picking and measurement uncertainty. A surveyed distance alone constrains scale; independently known points distributed in image radius and depth provide stronger lens constraints. Keep unknown scene points free, treat surveyed locations as measured constraints rather than truth invented from the solve, and anchor the gauge consistently: surveyed world constraints replace the free similarity gauge, so do not also freeze an incompatible initial pose. Optimize shared focal first, then radial terms only if edge coverage supports them. Hold out independent observations/points from fitting and evaluate their reprojection; a lower training residual can coexist with a worse lens. Cost: a small residual/Jacobian extension to the existing CPU solver, operator coordinate conventions, and artist survey data. No new model/GPU required for replayed tracks. Position and focal targets remain unverified until measured; if the demo has too few independent landmarks, report insufficient evidence instead of tuning to its ground truth.

3. **A dedicated self-calibration pass followed by a fixed-intrinsics solve.** DroidCalib jointly refines intrinsics within DROID's measurement loop; this can correct matches that were biased by the initial lens, whereas postprocessing a frozen wrong-lens track set may not. Its published calibration results and memory limits are in R20; its full benchmarks are not all a safe fit on this card and AGPL integration needs separate consideration. COLMAP's shared-camera BA [R22] is a classical alternative for textured, static, parallax-rich footage; the R20 comparison gives measured cost, not a local runtime prediction. GeoCalib [R21] provides an independent initialization and uncertainty check when geometry is weak, but its several-degree FoV errors are not a sub-percent promise. Once a lens passes held-out checks, rerun the existing fast tracker with it fixed. Cost: a second solve, separate dependencies and calibration coordinate conversion; CPU replay cannot establish new frontend timing. For zooming footage, segment constant-lens intervals or explicitly fit a regularized time-varying focal model; a shared focal cannot describe the shot.

**Why a verifier feedback loop needs care.** The verifier's detector/matcher and robust fit are not an end-to-end differentiable loss. Reuse its pixel observations in a separate numerical objective, keep the production verifier unchanged, and score unseen observations afterward. Record observability through a focal profile (optimize nuisance variables at fixed focal values), rank/conditioning and stability under observation splits; a small formal covariance based on correlated tracks is an optimistic lower bound. A degenerate shot should return “lens unresolved” and retain the supplied lens, rather than report certainty from a low residual.

Existing splat relighting/shadow support does not turn baked RGB/SH into physical albedo. A reconstructed splat can be an occlusion proxy while still being a poor shadow receiver. Separate **visual colour**, **confidence-masked geometric depth**, and **material/normal estimates**; validate holdout boundaries and shadows against the original plate before using them to constrain generation. Capture quality and parallax set the ceiling; a plausible hallucinated surface must remain marked as inferred.

### 4. Outside the box: three testable ideas

**A. Let failed verification produce a lens-correction proposal.** After any provider solve, persist the verifier's matched static landmark observations with their confidence and pixel conventions. Fit a shared focal/radial correction against independently known scene points, re-solving pose as needed; keep rejected matches and their reasons visible. The product would show the proposed lens delta and before/after *held-out* reprojection, then keep the previous solve available. First test on saved observations using CPU only, leaving the production verifier and acceptance thresholds unchanged. Benefit: all providers can share a calibration audit. Failure mode: training on the same landmarks used to award a pass, or allowing a bad object match to bend the lens. Reject proposals that improve fit points while harming hold-outs, or that have an unobservable focal/depth trade-off. No claim that the verifier itself is differentiable or that an automatic correction is already implemented.

**B. Track small, calibrate selected points at full resolution.** Solve the camera path at the economical working resolution, then revisit a deterministic set of high-confidence, edge-spanning static landmarks on original frames. Refine their locations at full resolution and run one shared-lens/pose adjustment. Freeze the crop/resize mapping and use the same principal-point convention; scale pixel focal appropriately, while converting radial coefficients through the actual lens normalization instead of blindly multiplying them. This targets the local resolution sensitivity without paying for a full-resolution neural solve. Measure both frontend and total runtime, held-out full-resolution reprojection and focal error. Failure mode: aliased low-resolution tracks point to the wrong physical feature; a high-resolution local search cannot fix an incorrect correspondence. Reject unstable or moving features. The proposed two-resolution comparison is a future experiment, not a measured speedup.

**C. Compile reconstructed geometry into confidence-aware holdouts and shadow receivers.** From a static background reconstruction, render depth/coverage into each accepted camera, retain only surfaces supported by consistent views, and derive a separate conservative proxy mesh for shadows. Feed the splat representation to visual/background rendering, the proxy to `ReadGeo3D`/holdout and shadow roles, and an explicit coverage mask to generation. Use the same surveyed world transform for all outputs so a cast shadow cannot slide relative to the plate. Begin with one surveyed static receiving surface and one inserted object; compare real plate edges, occlusion ordering and shadow contact through the shot. Failure mode: soft splat opacity and baked illumination become false geometry or double lighting. Keep splat colour out of the receiver's albedo estimate, do not fill unseen areas as measured surfaces, and reject ambiguous geometry. This bridges existing splat/mesh facilities; automatic extraction and physical material recovery remain unimplemented.

### 2. What would most change the artist's day

**Keying — reference-guided temporal alpha refinement (MatAnyone 2).** An artist supplies a target once; stable memory propagates subject identity while a learned human-matting prior recovers soft hair/detail, cutting per-frame garbage-matte work on difficult footage [CM1]. The matched YouTubeMatte improvement is evidence of a useful mechanism, not a measured win over ScreenKeyer. Keep ScreenKeyer's screen extraction/despill and artist core/garbage masks available; use the learned result as an optional boundary/foreground proposal, not an automatic replacement for glass, smoke or non-human keying. Honest cost: initial/corrective masks, display-to-model colour conversion, chronological analysis or saved state for scrubbing, unverified local peak/speed, and non-commercial default terms. For a permissive implementation with an aligned clean plate, Background Matting V2 [CM4] is the practical alternative; do not silently substitute its protocol or quality claims for MatAnyone 2's.

**Flow — a cached full-resolution MEMFOF analysis shared by motion consumers.** Large-displacement global matching plus the adjacent frame's evidence can improve occluded edges where local gradient flow struggles [CF1]. Its published full-HD memory headroom makes it a better first quality experiment than default VideoFlow, while NeuFlow v2 is the subsequent speed-oriented option. The existing VectorGenerator cache and named layers are the integration seam: change the analysis backend behind those contracts, not every downstream node. Honest cost: extra frame look-ahead, endpoint and cut policies, model/runtime packaging, source/colour/scale normalization, cold loading and GPU transfers, deterministic repeats, and checking both flow directions against their actual origin frames. Source-model uncertainty or a forward/backward residual alone cannot certify true visibility. Preserve the CPU reference and analytic-motion gates; benchmark all downstream warps, not just colour-wheel images. The proposed experiment below makes no production changes.

**Roto — corrective-keyframe SAM 2.1 assistance.** Start from the artist's mask, propagate through deforming/non-planar motion with the streaming memory, then accept a corrected keyframe and recompute only the affected interval [CR1]. This covers cases outside PlanarTracker's homography and rigid point-follow behavior. SAM 3.1's concept discovery and multiplexing are interesting for many objects, but do not justify its extra stack/licence burden before the simpler assistant is measured [CR2]. Honest cost: review of occlusion/reappearance, masks that chatter at hair/transparency, bounded per-shot state and recompute, and the distinction between raster-mask revisions and editable Bezier animation. Keep original Roto keys/feather intact; save prompts, model revision and derived-mask digests separately so a correction is reversible. No unattended contour-to-curve conversion and no promise of final-quality alpha from a segmentation score.

**Fluids preview — persisted, independently addressable display/refinement artifacts.** Choose **data access/layout**, not a new physical solver, for the stated playback/stop task. P3 spent **234.1 of 245.7 ms** fetching/evaluating; P4's documented profile was dominated by chain/geometry reconstruction, and its ring/texture reuse already brought playback below the existing **80 ms** gate [local baseline above, dated **2026-10-11/12**]. The remaining high-value technique is to bake/cache the stopped-frame liquid surface and whitewater representation with a direct manifest entry, then serve a bounded intermediate refinement without rebuilding source geometry or re-entering simulation. P4 already implements a ring, surface persistence and half-resolution refinement; the incremental task is proving/fixing the **old-cache dependency path**, not proposing those features as absent. Cost: extra persistent bytes, robust cache keys/invalidation, cancellation and progressive swap, and three-adapter evidence; old caches need a visible one-time migration or bounded fallback.

For genuinely **live re-simulation**, profile advection, particle transfers, pressure iterations, sparse occupancy and synchronization independently of rendering. Sparse tiles/multigrid/GPU residency are already built. NVIDIA Flow's block allocation and Warp's sparse kernels are useful design references [CS1,CS2]; a different pressure algorithm or SPH/LBM discretization is justified only if a measured solve bottleneck and matched physical-quality test support it. Learned surrogates require training and constrain the edit distribution [CS5,CS6]. None of those routes fixes the demonstrated old-cache stop by itself. Recover P4's raw record before calling its figures independently verified, and measure intermediate/full completion at the existing **frames 1, 60, 120** stop gate [P4, **2026-10-12**]. **Needs Gonzo:** route that existing finish work to the fluids owner; this research step starts no solver rewrite.

### 3. One worker-sized spike

[**MEMFOF against the current shared flow analysis**](../briefs/proposed/T3-memfof-shared-flow.md) is a proposed **90-minute** step with one pinned checkpoint, no production backend, matched full-HD inputs, existing analytic-motion gates, a large-motion quality test, an end-to-end two-direction speed target and bounded device memory. It explicitly accounts for MEMFOF's middle-frame flow origin: a triplet's two outputs do not equal the two directions of one adjacent pair. A failed speed/accuracy/resource gate produces an honest no-go record. This research run downloaded no weights and performed no card work; Gonzo owns any future assignment.

### 4. Outside the box: three testable ideas

**A. Corrective-keyframe roto as a reversible analysis layer.** Draw/accept one mask, propagate with SAM 2.1, and let the artist correct a later keyframe; cache masks only between the nearest accepted anchors and invalidate that interval when a prompt changes [CR1]. Store raster-mask revisions, source-frame/model digests and negative prompts beside the original Roto, preserving the authored Beziers and feather. A small future test compares correction time and boundary/identity failures against Planar follow on a deforming subject with an occlusion; a hard cut must start new memory. MatAnyone-style alpha refinement is a separate optional pass with its own licence [CM1]. This turns segmentation into revisable labor-saving analysis while leaving final edge decisions with the artist; success is less correction work at equal matte quality, not a high segmentation score alone.

**B. A provenance-aware motion analysis bus.** Build on the existing cached `vector.forward`, `vector.backward` and `vector.occlusion` layers: make analysis explicitly reusable across Retime/Kronos, Inpaint, paint propagation and tracker consumers, keyed by source digest, frame pair, colour transform, resolution and backend revision. The added idea is a shared validity/cost contract with confidence-triggered refinement, not inventing flow caching that already exists. A consumer can ask for a coarse answer during interaction and a pinned full-quality answer for render; the latter must never depend on an arbitrary cache warm-up order. A future test counts actual estimator invocations across a branching graph and checks downstream pixels/correct invalidation, including reversed frame pairs. Flow and visibility remain separate products; one mask does not suit all paint, interpolation and disocclusion policies.

**C. A learned residual cache over a physical preview.** Keep the deterministic low-resolution physical simulation as the source of timing, conservation and collider response, then learn a small shot-specific decoder for the difference between that preview and its baked high-resolution fields/surfaces. The latent can be a compact, interpolable playback artifact, inspired by Deep Fluids [CS5], with exact baked keyframes retained as anchors. First compare storage/decode cost, integrated density, collision/silhouette error and temporal artifacts against today's quantized display cache; train only after the current old-cache stop path is fixed. Interpolation at arbitrary display times is not physically accurate arbitrary-time re-simulation, and a source/collider edit invalidates the learned residual. Training amortization, decoder portability, versioning and cross-device repeatability may outweigh compression on a short shot; this is a later speculative experiment, not the answer to the current stop bottleneck.

### Progressive refinement is the product pattern

**Product target from the U1 brief, 2026-10-15:** first useful look **under one second**, progressively refine while the artist pauses; no silent blocking operation longer than **one minute**. These are proposed acceptance requirements, not achieved SDXL timings. Measure from the input event to the presented image, including queue, prepare/transfer, inference, decode and draw. Record cold startup separately and show the existing scene/last valid result immediately, visibly stale until updated. Never count that stale picture as a newly generated sub-second success.

| Mechanism | First look → refinement | What actually streams / limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Diffusion step sequence** [S1–S6] | Decode a scheduler-correct clean-image estimate after each completed step; replace provisional output until final decode. | Requires callback, VAE/preview decoder, asynchronous presentation and cancel/revision tokens. Raw noisy latent decoding is not necessarily meaningful. A few-step distilled schedule has fixed endpoints: do not claim arbitrary extra steps improve a completed image or splice **2-step** and **4-step** checkpoints into one chain. Decode overhead can erase the sampling gain. |
| **Optical-flow pyramid / iterative flow** [CF2,CF3,CF5] | Upsample coarse flow with vector units rescaled, show a provisional warp, replace with finer/refined flow. | Existing Lucas–Kanade/RAFT-style refinement exposes potential stages; released inference APIs often return only final flow. NeuFlow v2's small inference cost is the primary opportunity, not a promise of an existing streaming API. Publish confidence/occlusion provenance, not a falsely final vector bus. |
| **Path tracer / material-once rendering** [T1-C; 3D foundation] | First low-sample render, then accumulate samples with stable camera/material/light revision. | Genuine progressive accumulation; reset on changed scene, discard old-generation samples. **One viewer contract, different backends.** An edit receives a revision ID; retain the last valid image, cancel/coalesce superseded work, show a visibly provisional result, and promote only the matching final revision. Use a bounded newest-result queue, never accumulate one queued job per mouse movement. **Decision:** start with an instrumented few-step adapter and preview channel, then evaluate NeuFlow v2. Adopt the common interaction contract first; select each backend by its measured ability to honor it. A meaningful intermediate image is a visual/structural gate, not merely a decoded tensor arriving quickly.

### Ranking decision: speed before capacity before licence

**Agree with the proposed A → B → material-once sequence as an experiment order**, with a qualification: it is a decision about reducing artist wait on the current generative-shot workflow, not a claim that A already has the lowest measured latency. A tests the largest current wait with an existing control stack and creates the preview channel; B has stronger published speed evidence but solves a different subproblem. Material-once should ultimately offer the strongest live response to camera/light edits, after its unmeasured material/de-lighting setup succeeds. None has demonstrated the first-look target here.

- **A: [few-step SDXL plus displayed steps](../briefs/proposed/U1-few-step-sdxl-preview.md).** Fixed Lightning adapter, separately measured 12 GB offloaded / 48 GB resident variants, actual event-to-display gate. Recover stage-8 inputs; validate the distilled img2img schedule rather than assuming that truncating it preserves quality. Do not confuse step-count reduction with measured speedup.
- **B: NeuFlow v2 before MEMFOF.** Published **15 ms at 1024 × 436 on RTX 2080 fp16** [CF5, paper v2, checked 10/13] is a stronger first-look hypothesis than MEMFOF's **472 ms full-HD RTX 3090** [CF1]; the image sizes and direction contracts differ, so no direct speed ratio is asserted. Require a same-card **1080p, both-directions** rerun against **1.34 s** historical Lucas–Kanade [PARITY_2D.md V1, 2026-09-27], with analytic **<0.1 px** accuracy, disocclusion/large-motion testing, preserved vector units and full transfer/evaluator costs. Proposed first-look gate **<1 s**, not a published result. MEMFOF remains a quality-led fallback if NeuFlow misses accuracy.
- **C: T1-C material once, render every frame.** Amortizes generation and preserves scene camera/light geometry. First compare a supported object's de-lit material on known UVs; do not promise unseen texels or arbitrary scene reconstruction. Report material setup separately from per-frame render and invalidate progressive accumulation on edits.

Candidate promotion order is **credible first useful look**, then **eligible card/residency**, then **usable licence**; unknown latency is a measurement priority, never a fabricated numeric rank. Within the fast but unproven controlled-video group, Causal Forcing++/Causal-rCM deserve inspection before ordinary clip diffusion, but missing depth/camera checkpoints and licence gaps prevent automatic replacement. The top decision sheet carries the complete reordered T1–T3 queue.

#### What sets the bound

For a serial uncached result, event-to-picture includes event queue delay + snapshot/plan work + evaluation + GPU completion/readback + colour conversion/copies + paint/composition + scanout. Overlapped steady-state throughput is limited by the slowest stage and resource contention, while latency still includes queue depth. These are accounting identities, not measured stage timings. A fast GPU kernel does not bound total latency.

**Byte arithmetic (derived 2026-10-15, width × height × channels × bytes):** 3840×2160 RGBA8 is **33.18 MB**; RGBA32F is **132.71 MB**. At **60 Hz**, one full-image transfer is **1.99 GB/s** or **7.96 GB/s**, respectively (decimal units, unpadded). The current 3D viewport reads RGBA8; the float case illustrates an evaluator/display boundary, not an assertion about that viewport's format. A readback plus an equal-size upload doubles transferred bytes, but effective PCIe/USB4 bandwidth, staging, mapping waits and shared-memory adapters determine time. No theoretical link bandwidth is used to invent milliseconds. Qt's device-pixel-ratio model means **2×** scaling in each dimension gives **4×** pixels [V10]; the viewport explicitly applies that ratio before rendering. Measure actual physical render dimensions.

**Python/GIL:** CPython 3.12 permits one Python thread to execute interpreter code at a time [V11]. NumPy/native kernels can release the GIL, so Python is not a universal FPS ceiling. Many tiny dispatches, object allocation, document copies and Python paint callbacks can still occupy the UI's budget. Numba nopython + `nogil=True` releases the GIL for supported numeric code; object mode does not [V12]. Rust/PyO3 can release it around owned native data, but Python callbacks reacquire it [V13]. Neither compilation nor extra threads fixes a blocking presentation fence, an unnecessary recomputation, or a wrong cache key.

**QGraphicsView/event loop:** cache cheap static content, reduce unnecessary item/edge rebuilds, use exposed-region painting, and measure hit testing at realistic graph sizes. Qt documents cache/update-mode tradeoffs [V14]; QOpenGLWidget as a viewport changes the paint backend, not the number of Python callbacks or graph rebuilds. Avoid synchronous evaluation, waits and heavyweight property construction in input/paint handlers. A coalesced latest-state mailbox must discard stale work before it consumes expensive resources, while preserving the last valid image and interactive overlays.

#### Interop feasibility: prove a route before committing to it

**Prefer one graphics device, one presentation owner.** Direct wgpu screen presentation [V15] can avoid cross-API texture sharing altogether. Keep Qt chrome outside that native surface and draw image-space overlays in the surface, or prove composition/stacking explicitly. Keep bitmap fallback for unsupported headless/dock cases, visibly identified in measurements.

**Linux/Vulkan:** native texture wrapping through QRhi requires the texture to belong to its device or sharing context [V17]. Two unrelated VkDevices cannot exchange a raw VkImage. Separate-device sharing needs exportable allocation, compatible external-memory handle capabilities, import, layout/queue-ownership transitions and external semaphores/fences [V18]. POSIX descriptors are only handles; same physical adapter and compatible memory/format features remain gates. Wayland/X11 surface ownership is an additional presentation concern. Vulkan-to-GL requires an explicit supported memory/semaphore interop route; it is not implied by QOpenGLWidget.

**Windows:** choose a compatible backend explicitly—Qt's documented widget default is D3D11, not necessarily wgpu's D3D12 [V16]. Same-device native wrapping or D3D12 shared resources/heaps with NT handles and fences are possible mechanisms [V22]; format/state transitions and lifetime rules still apply. Cross-adapter sharing has restrictions and may use system memory, so it must not be advertised as free VRAM-local zero-copy. Matching the renderer to the display adapter may matter more than choosing the nominally faster GPU.

**Rust boundary:** wgpu exposes unsafe native/HAL texture construction [V21]; this is not a portable WebGPU or proven wgpu-py public texture-sharing API. A native bridge must reconcile its version, backend, device provenance and resource state with Qt. ### V1 part 3 — Three choices for DiMo

The schedules in these paragraphs are **V1 planning estimates, 2026-10-15**, not vendor promises. The target numbers refer to the budget definitions in part 2 and the acceptance contract in part 4.

**(a) Keep Python + Qt; fix presentation, scheduling and cached plans.** Preserve the graph, properties, document operations and CPU reference evaluator. **Week 1:** trace event-to-paint on the reported empty scene/checker and the QA shot; separate GUI scheduling from image work; stop doing synchronous rendering/readback inside the viewport paint callback; prove rendercanvas's direct screen surface on the display adapter with a retained checker texture, resize and overlays. Use **3–6 weeks** as the initial viewer-slice estimate, with profiling determining further evaluator work. **(b) Hybrid Rust/C++ render-and-present core behind the existing Python graph — recommended direction.** Let the native core own device, resident frame resources, bounded work queues, GPU colour/display transform, rendering and presentation; Python sends batched immutable edit commands and receives status, never a required full-frame array for ordinary display. Estimate **6–12 weeks** for the initial production slice; retain the changes from (a) even if the native boundary is deferred. This is my choice for “outperform the best” because it makes per-frame resource lifetime and scheduling explicit while retaining the feature investment. **(c) Replace the render loop and viewer in a native toolkit.** Build a native C++ Qt Quick/QRhi viewer or Rust/wgpu surface with native overlays and explicit presentation ownership; rendering chrome in the same wgpu pass avoids a cross-engine bridge but requires rebuilding text/input/accessibility integration. **Week 1:** inventory every viewer interaction and reference image, define document/command boundaries, and prove a standalone native image/view/overlay loop on both platforms. **Week 2:** implement a vertical slice with checker playback, camera orbit, selection and one Roto-handle edit, then run it against the existing behavior contract.
