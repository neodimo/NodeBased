# Volumes and liquids at interactive speed

**Lane 3 R5 · research date 2026-10-20.** Documentation/source inspection only. No weights downloaded, application changes, renderer execution or graphics-device work. Living sources below were accessed on this date; publication dates are stated when established. This is a scoped survey, not a latency leaderboard.

## Evidence and the question being answered

The R5 assignment (2026-10-20) supplies **26.69 s** to a **480 × 270, 1-sample** Hot pour image in a **1920 × 1080** viewport, and **112–756 s** for **960 × 540, 8–128 sample** frames on the **12 GB RTX 3080 Ti**, using a **64-cell** liquid surface and steam. These are **assignment-reported measurements, not independently verified here**. The inspected main revision, `15e28165`, still says the corrected sample matrix and viewport timings are outstanding in [FLUIDS_SPIKE, S2](FLUIDS_SPIKE.md#s2-of-2-the-hot-pour-reads-as-a-pour-in-progress-2026-10-15). Its corrected stills took **200.04–201.88 s at 32 samples**, and the older two-cup sweep describes a different composition. Recover the newer raw timing record before comparative acceptance; do not silently replace either run.

**Targets from the R5 assignment, 2026-10-20:** a fresh, useful warm first look **under 1 s**, then a settled image **under 60 s** on that card. Relative to the supplied times, the required improvements exceed **26.69×** for first look and **1.87–12.6×** for the frame range (derived by dividing by the target seconds; these are required reductions, not forecasts). First displayed approximation, first unbiased sample, final quality and cold preparation are different clocks. A stale image or an invisible completed array cannot pass the first-look gate.

## Part 1 — What production renderers actually do

No inspected source establishes a universal millisecond first image for arbitrary refractive liquids plus participating media on our card. The useful pattern is persistent preparation, an explicitly cheaper image during interaction, then progressively more faithful sampling. Even production products have cold stalls.

### Preparation and simulation-frame reuse

**Engineering synthesis from the sources above:** separate scene identity from camera identity. Keep mesh bottom-level acceleration, sparse density/temperature topology, conservative extinction bounds and device allocations resident across camera moves. Update instance transforms/top-level acceleration when needed. Liquid topology may change every simulated frame: refitting yesterday's triangle hierarchy is only valid while connectivity is unchanged and quality remains adequate. Changed topology requires rebuilding the affected hierarchy. Materials affecting extinction invalidate the relevant majorants; lights invalidate radiance, not necessarily geometry.

Persist independently addressable render artifacts with each simulation frame: surface topology/vertices, sparse fields, bounds, and proxy levels. A bounded adjacent-frame cache can hide decoding; it cannot reuse last frame's radiance as a converged result after density, geometry or lighting changes. Account for decode, upload and duplicate host/device copies. Offline conversion to NanoVDB may help repeat access but does not make conversion free or give WGSL a native NanoVDB reader.

### Coarse passes, skipping and tracking

**Density mips** lower sampling bandwidth for a deliberately approximate preview. Preserve thin steam and integrated extinction; averaged density is not a safe upper bound for rejection sampling. **Majorant hierarchies** instead store conservative extinction maxima including interpolation neighborhoods; traverse empty cells directly and sample tighter nonempty regions. These are different hierarchies and must not be substituted for one another. **Delta tracking** proposes extinction events and rejects null collisions; **ratio tracking** estimates transmittance with weighted null events. Tighter bounds reduce wasted density queries but traversal has a cost, and a high-density outlier can spoil a coarse cell. Deterministic shadow marching trades bias for bounded cost. Count work and image error before replacing it.

The production docs [N1] and papers [P1, P2] support these distinctions. Locally they suggest profiling shadow transmittance and existing majorant quality before implementing another sparse format. Bounds must cover temperature-driven emission even where density is low; skipping solely by density can erase fire. A coarse preview must retain the liquid silhouette, refraction/absorption cue and visible steam, while explicitly postponing expensive multiple scattering or caustics.

### Progressive samples, convergence and denoising

Accumulate independent samples into persistent sums/moments for an unchanged view; distribute work to uncertain pixels. A variance-derived standard error is an estimator, not proof that rare refractive paths or caustics have been observed. Require minimum samples, coverage checks and a high-quality reference; distinguish stopped-by-budget from converged. All image-space history must reset or be validity-tested after edits. Denoised output must never feed back into the unbiased accumulator.

**OIDN and OptiX at 1 spp:** [D1, D2] support fast denoising, but do not certify faithful single-sample smoke, glass or caustics for this shot. At one sample a path may miss a thin wisp entirely; filtering cannot recover reliable evidence. Surface guides can describe the glass boundary while the visible signal lies behind it. Compare beauty-only and appropriate albedo/normal guides, with explicit background/alpha handling. Keep raw output available and test temporal stability, steam retention and refracted detail. OIDN exposes CPU and multiple vendor device backends, persistent filters, quality choices and capability-dependent external-buffer sharing [D1]. OptiX is NVIDIA-specific [D2]. Their initialization, guide generation, transfers and inference belong in the measured budget; no matched 12 GB Ampere latency or memory peak was found in these sources. Neither is a drop-in WGSL operation.

## Part 2 — Fit to the app on fetched main

**Code evidence: main `15e28165`, read 2026-10-20; no runtime inference presented as measurement.** The assignment supersedes the retired historical L3 entry in `context/lanes.md` for this bounded documentation step. Rendering owns renderer changes; Fluids owns simulation/cache producer changes.

- [`pathtrace.py`](../nodebased/pathtrace.py), `_blas_of` and `build_scene`: mesh triangle/BVH preparation; shared geometry is deduplicated within a build. Its local dictionary does not persist across builds. The camera eye feeds splat normals; it is not grounds to rebuild camera-independent liquid triangles.
- [`gpupathtrace.py`](../nodebased/gpupathtrace.py), `reusing_scene`, `_scene_key`, `_prepared_scene`: scoped thread-local prepared-scene reuse includes scene identity, ambient, eye and volume identity, then drops the entry at scope exit. `_Frame.scene` reuses identical packed device arrays, but computing/comparing arrays can still cost host time. `_Frame.accumulator` reuses allocation and **clears samples**. Resource reuse already exists; persistent progressive sample reuse across calls does not follow from it.
- [`ptvolume.py`](../nodebased/ptvolume.py), `majorant_levels`, `free_flight`: **two-level** conservative local-majorant grids and delta tracking already exist. `coarse_majorants_sparse` builds bounds from sparse tiles. WGSL `vol_majorant`/free-flight code and `_pack_aux` implement the corresponding sparse atlas path. Adding generic “empty-space skipping” would duplicate shipped work. `transmittance` and the GPU shadow code still perform fixed-step midpoint shadow integration; this is a candidate for measurement, not evidence that ratio tracking is always faster.
- [`progressiverender.py`](../nodebased/progressiverender.py), `step`, `step_interactive`, and [`progressivejob.py`](../nodebased/progressivejob.py): coarse denoised previews, worker cancellation/restarts and convergence reporting already exist. Later steps retrace at increasing sample counts; guide/picture work can recur. Same-view continuation is the missing distinction from mere worker-thread progression. The adaptive variance estimator is already in `pathtrace.pixel_noise` and GPU adaptive passes.
- [`ptdenoise.py`](../nodebased/ptdenoise.py) is a spatial, edge-aware à-trous filter, not OIDN/OptiX. `pathtrace.guide_aovs` obtains guides and `denoised` handles background composition. Any denoiser replacement must respect alpha/holdouts and refracted content; a first-hit normal is not a complete description of the visible liquid/steam path.
- [`simcache.py`](../nodebased/simcache.py), [`FLUIDS_SPIKE.md`](FLUIDS_SPIKE.md) P4 and `tests.test_fluid_surface_cache`: persisted surfaces and decoded preview-ring work already exist. Extend the render-preparation handoff and invalidation keys, not a second simulation ring. CPU fallback is `pathtrace.render`/`ptvolume`; `liquid_render.py` is also a separate deterministic liquid ray-rendering path and must not be mistaken for the stochastic path tracer's `_liquid_event`.

### Effort, benefit model and portability

**All day ranges below are Lane 3 planning estimates dated 2026-10-20**, for an engineer familiar with this repository, including targeted correctness tests and measurement plumbing. They are not sourced vendor timings, promises or additive calendar schedules. **Absolute gains against 26.69 s / 112–756 s remain unknown without the missing phase profile.** Each row explains which term it can remove and what it cannot improve. The required reductions are in the opening section; no proposal is claimed to meet them yet.

| Technique / estimate | wgpu path and CPU fallback mapping | Expected gain against the supplied baseline | Radeon 8060S / llvmpipe risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Persistent preparation and simulation artifacts; **3–5 days** | Cache immutable mesh BLAS and volume preparation by revision, split camera-dependent splat work, retain bounded per-frame packed data; share preparation logic with CPU. Keep `_Frame` allocations. | Removes measured repeated build/pack/upload cost from warm views. If that cost is B seconds, potential first-look saving is at most B, not all 26.69 s. Also reduces preparation per refinement; cannot accelerate irreducible path work. | Moderate: host+device duplication, stale mutable arrays and driver buffer limits. llvmpipe shares host memory but duplicates still matter. No NVIDIA API required. |
| Budgeted coarse preview / density mips; **3–6 days** | Extend existing preview stages with cached proxy surface/volume levels; CPU uses the same LOD and explicit approximation mode. Keep faithful final scene separate. | Reduces first-pass path/density work; full-detail settle improves only by avoiding waste. Pixel scaling alone cannot remove B. Risk of false wins from erasing steam or lowering resolution until useless. | Moderate: divergence/workgroup tuning, thin-feature loss and software bandwidth. Must preserve holdouts, liquid cue and late full-quality replacement. |
| Improve existing majorant hierarchy; **2–4 days** | Instrument traversed cells, null events and density reads; optimize existing conservative bounds/interpolation halo. CPU remains reference. | Gains only the volume traversal fraction of first and settled frames. No numerical multiplier supported; sparse steam may benefit while dense steam may pay extra traversal. | Moderate: floating-point boundary disagreements and out-of-bounds indices. Test transformed/negative-coordinate grids and emission support on every adapter. |
| Ratio/residual-ratio shadow transmittance; **4–7 days** | Experimental estimator behind a toggle in `ptvolume.transmittance` and WGSL, using conservative bounds; retain midpoint reference. | Might reduce expensive shadow integration or improve bias at equal time; may increase variance and slow convergence. Both first-look and settled benefit unknown. | High correctness risk: overflow, null-event tails, estimator consistency and changed shadow noise. CPU equivalence by statistical confidence, not exact stochastic pixels. |
| Persistent sample sums + adaptive convergence; **4–7 days** | Resume sample indices, luminance moments and active mask for unchanged scene/view/output; CPU owns equivalent state. Reset on changed render identity/resolution/quality. | Reduces repeated full-resolution tracing. For a pure doubling schedule with unchanged estimator, cumulative work is less than twice the last pass (geometric-sum bound, derived here). Therefore removing retracing alone cannot explain the required 26.69× first-look reduction or the upper 12.6× settle reduction. Adaptive gains remain scene-dependent. | High: cancellation generations, sample independence, buffer lifetime and GPU/CPU variance matching. No NVIDIA-only dependency. |
| Persistent OIDN or optional OptiX denoise; **3–6 days**, excluding packaging/licence review | Compare against existing à-trous; warm device/filter, count guide generation/copies. CPU OIDN is the portable candidate; OptiX an optional NVIDIA route. | May lower samples needed for an acceptable preview/settle, but adds latency and cannot remove scene build. Single-sample refraction/volume fidelity unproved. No measured target-card saving. | High for zero-copy interop. OIDN HIP support does not establish this Radeon/driver combination; detect capabilities and keep CPU/existing fallback. llvmpipe cannot run OptiX. |
| Native NanoVDB representation; **7–12 days** | Convert/cache immutable frames, implement/bridge traversal and data layout for WGSL plus CPU; compare against current sparse atlas first. | Possible storage/access savings, offset by conversion and new traversal. No gain established over existing tiles; target-card costs unknown. | High: alignment/indexing, storage limits, extra representations and cross-vendor reader correctness. CUDA examples are not a portable wgpu implementation. |
| Temporal reservoir/radiance reuse; **10–20 days** | New validity/reprojection/reweighting machinery and histories; CPU reference estimator needed. | Potential later-frame convergence gain; cold/first edited frame still needs real work. Moving liquids, lighting edits and disocclusion invalidate much history. | Very high: history bias, ghosting, memory and driver divergence. Defer behind the ranked work below. |

For every row, published **12 GB Ampere cost is unavailable in the inspected sources**. An unknown benefit is not zero; it means run the stated measurement before adoption. Density mips and bounded previews trade fidelity explicitly. Final convergence should use the intended physical scene and avoid treating a sample cap as convergence.

### Hardware tiers and acceptance meaning

Hardware capacities here are **assignment-specified (R5, 2026-10-20)**, not newly probed:

- **RTX 3080 Ti, 12 GB:** primary performance gate; record peak allocation/residency as well as time. Stay within actual adapter storage limits, including guides, cache and duplicate frames.
- **RTX A6000, 48 GB:** repeat identical workload, then a separately labelled larger-scene capacity test. More memory permits a larger cache; no speedup factor inferred from capacity or the Ampere label.
- **Iris Xe + RTX 4070 laptop:** explicitly record physical render adapter, driver, backend and actual VRAM (capacity not assumed), plus which adapter presents. Measure event-to-visible-paint on the hybrid display path. An offscreen desktop timing cannot pass this tier. Retain CPU fallback if the chosen device is unsupported; don't silently measure CPU under an RTX label.
- **Radeon 8060S and llvmpipe:** correctness and bounded-memory/cancellation gates on both; publish their timings honestly rather than applying the NVIDIA latency result. `gpupathtrace.soft_supported` now enables AMD/RADV and llvmpipe, with a smaller workgroup for the mixed splat/volume shader. Its failure message still mentions NVIDIA-only support; use the actual predicate, not that stale string. Microsoft software remains gated. See code and [GPU testing requirements](../context/lanes.md), inspected 2026-10-20.
